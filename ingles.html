Internet connection technologies

Different technologies provide access to the internet. Availability, speed and quality may vary depending on location, infrastructure and the provider operating in a particular area.

Fiber Optic

Fiber optic technology uses light signals transmitted through optical cables. It can provide high speeds and reliable performance depending on the network and service plan.

Cable Internet

Cable internet uses cable networks to provide connectivity. Performance depends on the technology, network capacity and number of connected users.

Wireless Internet

Wireless connections transmit data through radio signals. Coverage and performance may be affected by distance, physical obstacles and environmental conditions.

Mobile Internet

Mobile internet uses cellular networks to connect compatible devices. Performance depends on coverage, network congestion and the terms of the selected service.

What should you check before choosing an internet service?

Availability

Contact the provider directly to confirm which technologies and plans are available at your address.

Connection Speed

Consider both download and upload speeds. Think about your needs for video calls, entertainment, studying and working.

Price and Conditions

Review the final price, installation fees, equipment costs, promotional periods and cancellation conditions.

Customer Support

Check the provider's official customer service channels, technical support and complaint procedures.

Basic connectivity concepts

What does Mbps mean?

Mbps stands for megabits per second. It is a unit used to describe the rate at which data is transferred over a connection.

What is latency?

Latency is the time it takes for data to travel from a device to a destination and receive a response. Lower latency is generally useful for video calls and online gaming.

What can affect Wi-Fi performance?

Distance from the router, walls, wireless interference, router placement and device capabilities can all affect Wi-Fi performance.
